Where clipping started: gaming

Gamers and streamers were the first clippers — cutting a funny play, a rage moment or a fail out of an hours-long stream. The scale is proven: in 30 days to 26 Aug 2026, six streamers generated 9,941 tagged clips from 1,820 accounts and just under 2 billion views (CreatorDB census, via report). Twitch says 80% of clips on Twitch are created by viewers — but the built-in Clip button only makes a clip; it does not pay anyone.

Cutting it anyway

  • Copyright infringement by default (17 U.S.C. §106)
  • Credit is not permission. Fair use is a court defence, not a slip.
  • YouTube: 3 active strikes = channel terminated. TikTok strikes expire after 90 days.
The gaming double-permission trap: a game clip has two owners — the publisher (game footage) and the streamer (their performance). Publisher fan policies let fans earn ad revenue, but none covers a paid campaign. For JRNY's own videos, permission is written into the brief; for anyone else's footage, get that owner in writing too.

The permission wording — copy it, adapt it, get a lawyer to check it

“You may cut clips only from the videos listed in this folder, only for this program, only for TikTok / Instagram Reels / YouTube Shorts / X, until [end date]. You may not use other videos of ours, other creators' videos, or copyrighted music. We may ask you to take a clip down at any time and you agree to do so promptly. This licence ends when the program ends.”

Example wording (research report §3) — not legal advice. Have a lawyer check your brief for your country.

Permission does NOT fix platform originality rules

YouTube deprioritises channels that mainly repost others' videos “without adding anything of your own” — its reused-content policy applies even with permission. Instagram (Apr 2024) recommends only the original when it finds identical videos. Fix: require a real edit — own hook, commentary, a different cut, captions — not a plain re-upload.

Lifetime means lifetime

When someone signs up through your link and trades, you earn a % of that person's trading fees for as long as that person trades — not once, not for 30 days. Build a base of traders and the income keeps coming in passively. Put the lifetime term in writing in the brief, with the exact % and when it's claimable.

How the link works

Every poster gets their own tracked link and code from day one — in bio, plus a spoken “link in bio” and the code on screen (TikTok captions aren't clickable). Tracking can't be added to a post that's already live. A post may also earn the platform's own rewards — paid by TikTok, YouTube or Instagram itself, to the poster's own account; JRNY does not pay, top up, or owe them. Platform pay alone is tiny — the lifetime fees are the real money.

The fake-view and dispute rules bite hardest on per-view campaigns. JRNY funds none — but his posters still earn, so disclosure law applies in full.

Paid cuts are ads — disclose them

Under the FTC Endorsement Guides (2023), payment/commission must be disclosed clearly in the video itself, near the start — not buried in hashtags or the description, and the platform toggle alone is not enough. The advertiser must train and monitor posters. TikTok's Branded Content Policy (31 Aug 2026) requires its toggle for any paid promotion. For affiliate links, say “I get commissions for sign-ups through links in this post.”

Fake views & cut farming

Paying per view invites bots; the FTC's 2024 rule bans fake influence indicators. Signs: views spike then stop; generic repeated comments; wrong geography; near-identical cuts at once; views climb while sign-ups stay flat. Hold payment on 2+ signs until a human reviews.

Payment disputes

On per-view campaigns: only reject for a reason written in the brief in advance; budgets run out — say so upfront; paying late is “the fastest way to lose your best clippers” (Salvo). JRNY has no per-view payments, so no per-view disputes — his only money term is the affiliate %, in writing.

Scam warning for clippers

A real campaign never charges anyone to join, never asks for a password or phone code, and never pays only in gift cards. Entry fees are the clearest red flag.

What we couldn't verify — open questions

  • No Solana / memecoin launchpad program like this was found in the research. The setup is adapted from streamer, game and crypto practice.
  • Rates and platform rules change fast — check the live platform rules before you launch.
  • TikTok link mechanics: the bio-link + “link in bio” + code workaround was not verified against a primary TikTok source — test it first.
  • Legal/tax: general information, not legal advice. Rules vary by country.
